Photosynthesis

How green plants make their own food using light energy.

The big idea

Photosynthesis is the process in which green plants use light energy to turn carbon dioxide and water into glucose. Oxygen is released as a product.

carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen
light energy is captured by chlorophyll
Easy way to remember: The plant takes in CO₂ and water, uses sunlight as the energy source, makes glucose for itself, and releases O₂.

Where does it happen?

Photosynthesis mainly happens in the leaves, especially in cells containing many chloroplasts.

Chlorophyll is the green pigment inside chloroplasts. It absorbs light energy from sunlight.

  • Chloroplast = organelle where photosynthesis occurs.
  • Chlorophyll = pigment that captures light energy.
  • Leaves contain many photosynthesising cells.

Water travels from the roots to the leaves through the plant's transport tissues. Carbon dioxide enters leaves through tiny openings called stomata.

Diagram: follow the ingredients

Think of the chloroplast as the plant's tiny solar-powered food factory.

Inside a leaf

The palisade layer is near the top of the leaf and contains many chloroplasts, so it is an important place for photosynthesis.

The spongy layer has air spaces that help carbon dioxide move around inside the leaf.

Stomata are tiny pores that allow gases to move in and out.

What happens to extra glucose?

Plants use glucose for respiration and for building other substances. When they make more carbohydrate than they need immediately, they can store it as starch.

Starch test: iodine solution turns blue-black when starch is present.

Why oxygen matters

Oxygen is produced during photosynthesis and can diffuse out of the leaf through the stomata.

Experiments

  • Variegated leaf: only green areas contain chlorophyll, so these areas are expected to form starch when conditions are suitable.
  • Opaque cover: covering part of a leaf blocks light. The exposed part can be compared with the covered part after testing for starch.
  • Leaf-disc experiment: oxygen bubbles can make leaf discs rise. More bubbles generally mean more oxygen is being produced.
Quick check — Photosynthesis

1. Which substance captures light energy?

Answer: Chlorophyll. It is the green pigment that absorbs light energy.

2. Which gas enters a leaf as a raw material for photosynthesis?

Answer: Carbon dioxide. It enters through stomata.

3. Why can starch be found in a leaf?

Answer: Starch is a storage carbohydrate made from glucose produced by photosynthesis.

The Carbon Cycle

Carbon atoms move between the atmosphere, living things, soil, oceans and long-term stores.

What is carbon?

Carbon is an element with the symbol C. It is found in many compounds that make up living things, including carbohydrates, proteins and fats.

Important: An organism does not normally use carbon as a pure element to build its body. Carbon is transferred in the form of carbon-containing compounds.

Photosynthesis puts carbon into plants

Plants take in carbon dioxide from the air. During photosynthesis, the carbon atoms from CO₂ become part of glucose and other carbon compounds.

6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂

The carbon atom has not disappeared. It has simply moved into a different compound.

Carbon in living organisms

  • Plants contain carbon in their tissues.
  • Animals get carbon by eating plants or other animals.
  • Decomposers such as bacteria and fungi obtain carbon from dead organisms and waste.

Carbon can move through living organisms, the atmosphere, decomposers, soil and long-term stores.

Respiration returns CO₂

Plants, animals and many other organisms respire. During aerobic respiration, glucose reacts with oxygen and carbon dioxide is produced.

glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water + energy

Plants respire too — including at night.

Decomposition returns carbon

When organisms die or produce waste, decomposers break the material down. Carbon compounds are used by decomposers and carbon dioxide can be released during their respiration.

Think of decomposers as nature's recycling team. They help move matter back into the environment.

Combustion and fossil fuels

Coal, oil and natural gas are fossil fuels. They contain carbon that was stored over very long periods.

When fossil fuels are burned, carbon in them combines with oxygen and carbon dioxide is released into the atmosphere.

Minerals and plant growth

Magnesium (Mg²⁺)

Plants need magnesium to make chlorophyll. A shortage can cause leaves to become yellow because less chlorophyll is produced.

Nitrate (NO₃⁻)

Nitrate ions are needed to make amino acids and proteins. A shortage can lead to poor growth and smaller, paler plants.

The carbon cycle in one sentence

Carbon dioxide → plants → food chains → respiration/decomposition → carbon dioxide, with some carbon also stored for long periods in places such as fossil fuels.
Quick check — Carbon Cycle

Answer: Respiration releases carbon dioxide.

Answer: Fossil fuels contain carbon stored over very long periods.

10-question challenge

1. What is the main energy source for photosynthesis?

Answer: Sunlight.

2. Which organelle contains chlorophyll?

Answer: Chloroplast.

3. What gas is a raw material for photosynthesis?

Answer: Carbon dioxide.

4. What gas is released as a product of photosynthesis?

Answer: Oxygen.

5. What is glucose used for?

Answer: Glucose can be used in respiration and to make or store other substances.

6. Which process moves carbon from the atmosphere into plant biomass?

Answer: Photosynthesis.

7. How do animals usually obtain carbon?

Answer: Animals obtain carbon through their food.

8. Which organisms help break down dead material and waste?

Answer: Decomposers such as bacteria and fungi.

9. What happens when fossil fuels are burned?

Answer: Combustion releases carbon dioxide into the atmosphere.

10. Which statement is correct?

Answer: Plants can photosynthesise and also respire.

Exam-ready summary

  • Photosynthesis uses light energy to make glucose from carbon dioxide and water.
  • Chlorophyll captures light energy inside chloroplasts.
  • Oxygen is released during photosynthesis.
  • Glucose can be used in respiration or converted into storage substances such as starch.
  • Carbon enters food chains through plants and moves between organisms when they feed.
  • Respiration and decomposition return carbon dioxide to the atmosphere.
  • Burning fossil fuels releases stored carbon as carbon dioxide.
  • Plants need mineral ions such as magnesium and nitrate for healthy growth.
